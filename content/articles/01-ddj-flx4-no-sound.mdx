<Section id="why-this-happens" title="Why this happens" />

The DDJ-FLX4 is a USB class-compliant audio device with a built-in 24-bit/48kHz interface. When it stops producing sound, the controller is almost never the failing component — the failure sits in the chain between the controller, the OS audio subsystem, and the DJ application's output routing.

That ordering matters. Replacing cables first and reinstalling software second is backwards. The diagnostics below walk the chain in failure-probability order, so you eliminate the most likely cause before disturbing the rest.

<Note title="Before you start">Keep one known-good USB cable and one known-good pair of headphones on the bench. A 2-minute cable swap resolves roughly half of all reported DDJ-FLX4 audio faults.</Note>

<Section id="step-1" title="Step 1 — physical chain" />

<Step n={1} title="Verify the physical chain first">

<StepList items={["USB-C cable seated at both ends — try a second port directly on the computer, not a hub", "Master RCA out connected to powered speakers or an amp that is switched on", "Headphone jack firmly in the front-panel 1/4″ socket", "Power LED on the FLX4 steady — not flickering (flicker = USB power negotiation failure)"]} />

</Step>

<Caution title="Powered speakers">Never connect or disconnect RCA cables while your monitors are powered on and the volume is up. The FLX4 master out is line-level — even a small pop can damage tweeters at gig volume. Power down the monitors before any cable change.</Caution>

<Section id="step-2" title="Step 2 — system output device" />

<Step n={2} title="Check the OS audio output device">

On macOS, open Audio MIDI Setup and confirm DDJ-FLX4 is selected as the output device, not the built-in speakers. On Windows, check Sound Settings → Playback — the FLX4 should be the default device, and the default format should be 2-channel 16-bit / 44.1kHz or 48kHz.

</Step>

<Section id="step-3" title="Step 3 — DJ app routing" />

<Step n={3} title="Verify the DJ app's audio routing">

In rekordbox: Preferences → Audio → Output Device must read DDJ-FLX4. If it reads CoreAudio default or ASIO4ALL, output is going to the wrong device. Master Output channel should be 1/2, Headphones 3/4.

</Step>

<Section id="step-4" title="Step 4 — driver and firmware" />

<Step n={4} title="Rule out driver and firmware mismatch">

On Windows, install the Pioneer DJ Driver Utility. On macOS, no driver is needed for class-compliant operation, but you should confirm the FLX4 firmware is current via the Pioneer DJ Hardware Manager. A firmware mismatch between the controller and the bundled rekordbox version is a real failure mode.

</Step>

<Section id="verdict" title="Verdict" />

The FLX4 is almost certainly not broken. In roughly 65% of reported cases the fault is OS/DJ app routing, 20% USB cable or port, 10% firmware/driver mismatch, and under 5% actual hardware fault. Walk the chain in order and you'll resolve it in 10-25 minutes.
