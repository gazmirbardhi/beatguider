## Why Stems Changed Everything for DJs

**Live stem separation is, in my mind, the most important and impactful innovation we've seen in DJ technology over a 13-year period.**

I play music in a completely different way today thanks to stems. The funny part is I was really cynical about them at first, and I think rightly so.

The first generation of stem separation delivered by Virtual DJ and Algorithm was frankly just a bit rubbish. I hadn't counted on how quickly things would develop, though.

By the time Serato entered the fray alongside the second generations of the tech from those other two platforms, we had got to the point where stems were actually usable.

<Note title="The 13-Year Leap">

From unusable first-generation separation to modern AI-powered real-time stems, the pace of improvement has been extraordinary. What started as a gimmick is now a core feature in every major DJ platform. The 2025 generation of stem tech is the first where every platform is genuinely usable in a live setting.

</Note>

---

## The Honest Truth About Stem Quality

**Even today, no stem separation tech on the market completely matches the quality of true studio-sourced stems files.**

It seems unlikely it ever quite will. But what I realized a while ago is that good enough can actually be good enough, especially when DJing.

It's rare you'll ever be playing one stem solo. So whilst a vocal for example might not sound amazing in isolation, when layered over the instrumental of another track, the other sounds present will do a lot to hide the flaws.

<Note title="Pro Tip: The 'Good Enough' Rule">

If a stem sounds 80% clean in isolation, it will sound 95% clean in a mix. Don't let perfect be the enemy of good — some of the best mashups come from stems that don't sound amazing on their own but work perfectly when layered. Test your stems in context, not in isolation.

</Note>

## How This Test Was Set Up

**I purchased two lossless AIFF files from Beatport to give the algorithms the best chance of success, as opposed to using lossy MP3s.**

The track chosen for this test is deliberately complicated. The drums are doing quite a lot, there are real instruments, there's a full bassline, and there are two types of vocal — a rap and a sung vocal — plus some scratching. None of them are going to get this perfect. This is a difficult piece of music, but that's exactly why it was chosen.

This is by no means a true scientific test, but I did my best to keep things as fair as possible. Beatport streaming does offer lossless audio and can be used with on-the-fly stems in all DJ software, but Engine DJ can't do stems with streaming music at all.

For the DJ software — Rekordbox, Serato DJ Pro, Algoriddim DJ Pro, Virtual DJ, and Traktor — I fed straight out of the apps directly via Rogue Amoeba's Loopback and recorded the outputs as **24-bit WAV files**, which I then normalized to the same levels.

Where there was an option, I chose the highest quality stems possible and disabled any extra audio processing like Traktor's Ozone Maximizer. Virtual DJ does offer the ability to change the bleed settings between stems, which is a cool feature, but I left that on the default setting for this test.

Engine DJ stems can only be used on device, so I recorded those internally on the Rane System 1, again to WAV. For all of the samples, the audio has been lossless right up until the final export for YouTube, at which point it is converted to **320K AAC**.

### Test Structure

Each platform was tested across multiple stem configurations to give a thorough, comparable result across all systems. The test ran through each platform in the same order: first the instrumental with vocals removed, then drums and bass together, then just the drums, and finally the acappella — which includes the singing, the scratching, and the rap.

The acappella is always the bit these systems are going to struggle on the most. It's a really good test for all the systems here.

<Note title="Test Methodology at a Glance">

- **Source files**: Lossless AIFF from Beatport (not lossy MP3)
- **Recording**: 24-bit WAV via Rogue Amoeba's Loopback
- **Normalisation**: Levels matched across all platforms

- **Engine DJ**: Recorded internally on Rane System 1

## How Traktor Handles Stems Playback

**The weird thing with Traktor is you have to analyze ahead of time — you can't do it in real time.**

What it does is create a stems file. When it plays back all of the stems at once, it's playing not the original track but the combination of all four stems put together, which does not sound the same as the original.

With Engine, when you've got all four stems live, it's playing the original file. As soon as you deactivate a stem or solo a stem, it switches over to the stems file. But once you go back to all four stems playing at once, you're back to the original track — so there's no quality loss.

Neuo Stems for Traktor does have an option to do some kind of AI render, which is actually going to give you a better quality when you've got all four stems playing at once in Traktor. However, I do think the stems quality in Traktor is generally pretty good, so that's one thing in its favour.

### What is live stem separation and why does it matter for DJs?

Live stem separation is the most important and impactful DJ technology innovation of the last 13 years. It allows DJs to isolate individual elements of a track — vocals, drums, bass, and instruments — in real time during a performance. This makes it possible to create mashups, acapella outs, live remixes, and bootlegs that would have been impossible with traditional DJ gear. Combined with key shifting, key matching, looping, sync, and effects across four decks, it's now possible to basically produce music on the fly.

#### How do DJ stems work technically?

Stem separation uses AI and machine learning algorithms trained on millions of audio samples to identify and isolate different sound elements within a mixed track. The algorithm analyses frequency content, harmonic structure, transient patterns, and stereo placement to separate drums, bass, vocals, and instrumentation into independent audio streams. Most DJ software does this in real time (on-the-fly), while some platforms like Engine DJ and Traktor require advance analysis to generate a stems file before playback.

#### Do stems sound as good as studio-sourced acapellas?

No — even the best stem separation tech in 2025 does not completely match the quality of true studio-sourced stems files. It seems unlikely it ever quite will. However, good enough is often good enough when DJing. It's rare you'll ever play one stem solo — when you layer a vocal stem over another track's instrumental, the other sounds present will do a lot to hide the flaws. The improvement from first-generation stem separation (Virtual DJ and Algorithm) to today's second and third-generation tech has been massive.

#### Which DJ platform has the best sounding stems in 2025?

Algoriddim djay Pro on Mac ranked first in the 2025 head-to-head test for overall natural sound quality, clear hi-hats with no pumping, and excellent sound staging. Virtual DJ came second with the most believable vocals and drums across all variations. Traktor DJ placed third with consistent all-round quality and a strong acappella. Serato was fourth, Rekordbox fifth, and Engine DJ sixth — though all were deemed passable and usable.

#### Which platform is best for drum-focused stem mixing?

Serato DJ Pro stands as the best when it comes to drums. The drum stems have a distinctive character that many DJs prefer for drum-focused mixing and beat juggling. In testing, Serato delivered drums with a different interpretation than other platforms — less bass bleed in the drum channel, which some DJs may prefer depending on their mixing style.

#### Which platform is best for vocal stem separation?

Virtual DJ has had the reputation as being the best for vocals, and testing confirms that position. Its vocal stems are more believable with less warble and distortion compared to most competitors. The platform appears to make a deliberate compromise — letting a tiny bit of bleed through in order to keep the vocals sounding natural. In the 2025 rankings, Algoriddim djay Pro edged out Virtual DJ for first place overall, but Virtual DJ remains the top choice for vocal-heavy sets.

#### How should I choose a stem platform for my workflow?

The clear winner is the one you've got access to in your DJ software. If you're playing off Rekordbox USBs, your options are limited to what that hardware supports. If you want the best possible stem quality and don't mind preparing in advance, Neuo Stems v4 is a $34 app that can generate stems files for Traktor and Serato with superior quality. For on-the-fly use, all major platforms (Rekordbox, Serato, Virtual DJ, Traktor, Engine DJ, Algoriddim djay Pro) offer acceptable real-time stem separation.

#### Does the source material affect stem quality?

Yes — significantly. A track with clean, well-separated elements will produce much better stems than a dense, heavily layered mix. In testing, the first sample track with cleaner vocals produced fantastic results on some platforms, while a second track with more complex instrumentation and overlapping frequencies caused all platforms to struggle. Each stem separation tech has its own strengths and weaknesses that interact differently with different source material. The best approach is to test your actual tracks on the platform you plan to use.

#### What is the difference between on-the-fly stems and prepared stems?

On-the-fly stems are processed in real time as you play — you load a track and the software separates the elements instantly. This is convenient but typically produces lower quality results. Prepared stems are analysed in advance (either by the DJ software or by a dedicated app like Neuo Stems) and saved as a stems file. This allows higher quality separation algorithms to run without time constraints. Engine DJ and Traktor require advance preparation. Rekordbox, Serato, Virtual DJ, and Algoriddim djay Pro offer on-the-fly separation.

#### What is Neuo Stems and how does it compare to built-in software stems?

Neuo Stems v4 is a $34 standalone app that produces higher quality stem separation than the built-in algorithms in most DJ software. It can export stems as individual files or package them as native stems files for Traktor and Serato. The app offers two processing algorithms and until recently only worked with Traktor, but now supports Serato too. In testing, Neuo Stems produced superior results to all built-in DJ software options, making it the best choice for DJs who want maximum quality and are willing to prepare tracks in advance.

#### Are there any free stem separation tools?

Yes — Ultimate Vocal Remover (UVR) is a free, open-source stem separation tool with loads of different algorithms to choose from. It only separates vocals and instrumental (not individual drums, bass, and instruments), and requires export and manual loading into your DJ software. Results vary significantly depending on which algorithm you choose. It's a solid budget option for DJs who want to experiment with stems without spending money, but the quality won't match paid solutions like Neuo Stems or dedicated DAW tools.

#### Do DAW-based stem tools work with DJ software?

You can't use DAW stem separation (like Ableton Live 12's built-in tool, Lalal.ai, or Rip X DAW) inside DJ software in real time. However, you can export the vocal stem from any of these tools and load it into your DJ software as an acapella track. This gives you access to higher quality separation than most DJ apps offer, at the cost of advance preparation. Lalal.ai with its V6 Andromeda algorithm and Ableton Live 12 were included in testing and both showed some artefacts but generally strong separation quality.

#### How has stem quality improved in the last year?

There has been a really big leap forward — the 2025 test is at least the third year of running these comparisons, and the improvement in quality is significant. None of the platforms tested were bad — all were passable. The number of platforms with built-in stems has also grown. Engine DJ and Traktor weren't part of the test the previous year. Modern DJ systems now give access to every acapella that ever existed, opening up mashups, bootlegs, remixes, and re-edits that would have been a complete pipe dream for DJs back in the day.

#### How does Engine DJ handle stems differently?

Engine DJ Standalone works differently to all other platforms. It doesn't process stems in the unit itself — it analyses beforehand on a laptop and produces a special stems file that travels with you to the Engine DJ equipment. The end result is the same in terms of playback, but the implementation is different. When all four stems are playing together, Engine DJ plays the original track file — there's no quality loss. As soon as you deactivate or solo a stem, it switches to the stems file. When you bring all four back, it reverts to the original. Engine stems are usable but lagged behind everything else in 2025 testing.

#### Does Traktor's stems playback degrade the original audio?

Yes — there's a quirk. Traktor requires advance analysis which creates a stems file. When it plays back all four stems at once, it plays the combination of all four stems rather than the original track, which does not sound identical to the original. Neuo Stems for Traktor has an AI render option that improves quality when all four stems play together. However, Traktor's stem quality is generally pretty good, so this is a manageable trade-off for most DJs.

#### Why did Rekordbox score lower than expected in the ranking?

Rekordbox has gone from three stems to four, catching up with everyone else, and its latest generation is actually pretty good. But in the 2025 head-to-head test, the acappella had a specific issue — during the rap section referencing 'Technics 1210s,' it cut off the 'T,' rendering it as 'echnics 1210s.' That was the deciding factor placing it at 5th. For general instrumental work, Rekordbox is solid and slightly superior to Engine DJ. It also lacks the drums-and-bass-together stem configuration that other platforms offer.

#### Can I mix stems across different platforms?

Yes — with some effort. You can use a dedicated app like Neuo Stems to generate stems files that work natively in Traktor and Serato. For Rekordbox USB-based systems, you'd need to use a DAW tool (Lalal.ai, Ableton Live, Rip X DAW) to export individual stem files and load them as separate tracks. There's no universal stems file format that works across all platforms, but with advance preparation you can use any stem separation tool and inject the results into your preferred DJ software.
