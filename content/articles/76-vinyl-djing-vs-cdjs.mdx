## What Information Does a CDJ Give You That Vinyl Doesn't?

**When you load a track into a CDJ, you have got information at your fingertips like the BPM readout, the key information, and even the waveform.**

If you're not on a CDJ, this will be on your laptop screen. Even just that information I've just explained is so so powerful.

The other piece of information that you don't get on a vinyl turntable is how much the tempo is adjusted by. If I move the tempo slider you adjust that and you get a readout of as to how much you have adjusted the speed of the record by. That's really really quite powerful.

Then of course within the waveform, because of the technology built into the CDJ, you can also store cue points and multiple cue points at that.

**On a vinyl turntable you have absolutely none of that — you essentially have a record on a platter which is basically ready to go.**

You can adjust the speed of the record depending on if it's a single or an album, sort of by 33 RPM or 45 RPM — that's just how quickly the turntable moves. You've got the target light so that will help you cue up your tracks, and it lights up the vinyl a bit.

In the bottom right hand corner you have got a tempo adjust but no readout. ## How Cueing Works on Vinyl: Finding Your Cue Point Manually

**On the CDJ the cue loaded straight away at the start of the track and you can basically hit play and you're ready to go — the same cannot be said about a vinyl turntable.**

You have to actually find your cue point manually, and you would do this by moving the record. If we want the cue point to be at the start of the record, you find the first beat, and it's basically a case of releasing the record when you want it to start.

This is why a lot of vinyl DJs who knew their records inside out had little stickers all over their records. Because that is their way of locating the cue point.

<Section id="faq" title="Frequently asked questions" />

#### Is DJing on vinyl harder than using CDJs?

Yes — beat matching on vinyl requires you to find cue points manually, match BPMs by ear, and physically manipulate the platter while adjusting the tempo slider. CDJs provide BPM readouts, waveform displays, and instant start, making the mixing process significantly faster and easier.

#### Do you need a BPM counter to beat match on vinyl?

No — vinyl turntables do not have BPM readouts. You must beat match entirely by ear, listening for drift between the two tracks and adjusting the tempo slider and platter accordingly. This is one of the main reasons vinyl DJs develop such strong ear training.

#### Can you use key lock or master tempo on vinyl turntables?

No — vinyl turntables do not have key lock or master tempo features. When you adjust the tempo slider, the pitch of the track changes as well. This means you have to rely on your ears not just for timing but also for key compatibility between tracks.

#### What is the advantage of CDJs over vinyl?

CDJs provide BPM readouts, key information, waveform displays, saveable cue points, instant start, and master tempo. These features allow DJs to focus on more complex techniques like looping, hot cues, and effects rather than spending all their energy on basic beat matching.

#### Why are vinyl DJs so passionate about the craft?

Vinyl DJing requires countless hours of practice to master beat matching by ear, platter manipulation, tempo adjustment without readouts, and knowing your records inside out. This deep investment of time and effort creates a strong connection to the craft that vinyl DJs are very protective of.

#### What is better for a beginner — vinyl or CDJs?

For beginners, CDJs or controllers are much more accessible. The BPM readouts, visual waveforms, and instant start make learning to mix significantly easier. Vinyl is a more challenging and rewarding path if you want to develop deep ear training and manual skills, but it requires a much steeper learning curve.

#### Can you use cue points on a vinyl turntable?

Not in the same way as a CDJ. On vinyl, you must find your cue point manually by moving the record with your hand. Many vinyl DJs used small stickers on their records to mark cue locations. There is no saveable, instant-jump cue point system like on CDJs.

#### Do vinyl turntables have a tempo readout?

Vinyl turntables have a pitch slider with markings (2, 4, 6, 8) but no digital readout of the exact percentage. There is no BPM counter either. You must rely entirely on your ears to determine how much you've adjusted the tempo.
